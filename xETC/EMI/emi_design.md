### Active learning task in class

In class we have talked about how lectures can be made more interactive to help students to engage with the ideas being presented.
You will add one active learning task to your lecture. Consider how this task will benefit your students – what do you hope they will learn from it?

Consider the following for your active learning task:
* Is the active learning task relevant to the learning goals?
* Is the active learning task engaging for students?
* Do students have enough knowledge to complete the active learning task effectively?
* What instructions do you need to give the students to complete this task?
* How will you measure the effectiveness of this task?

## Effective lecture
* To start:
  * during the first lecture, introduce yourself to the students, telling them of your connection with the subject matter
  * give an overview of what you will be covering and how, in the lecture series
  * specify your expectations in relation to dealing with questions or concerns
  * make explicit the relevance of the topic to the course, students' future careers, and current real -life context
  * specify the objectives of the particular lecture
  * specify the structure and organisation of the lecture
  * determine your students' initial starting point in relation to the topic, perhaps by use of a 'knowledge probe' or quick quiz which assesses students' initial level of knowledge

* Presentation
  * communicate your enthusiasm for the topic; assume interest and enthusiasm on the part of the students
  * you may not be able to control whether you feel confident, but you can act confident
  * fit a time frame to your structure and vary your pace if required
  * limit the information on each PowerPoint slide and be wary of technology that distracts (eg fly-ins, sound effects)
  * ensure legibility of slides in terms of font size and style and colour contrast between text and background
  * avoid PowerPoint 'karaoke' (reading aloud from the PowerPoint slide), but make use of slides to help students from different language backgrounds match key words and concepts with your pronunciation of these

* Concluding the lecture
  * returning to the initial outline and reviewing it
  * rounding off with a 'take-home message'
  * identifying unfinished business and indicating how it might be dealt with
  * formulating an agenda for the next lecture
  * setting a (small) task related to the next lecture

## Signposting in EMI

In education, signposting refers to the use of verbal cues, transitional phrases, or written structural outlines that guide students through a lesson, presentation, or academic text. It provides a conceptual "roadmap", helping learners understand where they are, what they are about to learn, and how the current material connects to the broader goals of the course.

Use signposting language to:
* Introduce a topic and establish its relevance
* State your purpose
* Announce your lecture structure
* Conclude and summarise the main points
* Refer to the previous lesson
* Move from one point to another
* Make a backward reference
* Emphasise key points
* Provide examples
* Refer to visual material
